Sector screener

Small US companies that look cheap and are still healthy. Pick a sector to see every company that passes all eight checks, best score first.

 

What a company needs to pass
  • Price to sales under 2You pay less than $2 for each $1 of yearly sales.
  • Market value under $3 billionSmaller companies get less attention, so mispricings last longer. Under $2 billion earns extra points.
  • Long-term debt under half of equityTop marks go to companies with more cash than debt, and the best case is no debt at all. A company whose debt can't be read reliably from its filings can't pass this check. If the debt that could be read is already over the limit, it fails. Otherwise, if it passes all the other checks, a note under the results names it.
  • Net profit margin of 8% or moreAt least 8 cents of profit on every sales dollar, not counting one-time items. One-time gains, such as selling a business, are taken out, one-time charges, such as a write-down or a loss on paying off debt early, are added back, and an unusual tax bill is replaced by a normal one. Gains, charges and tax credits that come most years count as part of the business.
  • Based and incorporated in the USRules out foreign listings (ADRs) and companies registered offshore, such as in the Cayman Islands.
  • Positive equity and free cash flowIt owns more than it owes, and it brings in more cash than it spends.
  • Profitable in 2 of the last 3 yearsOne good year can be luck. Two out of three is a pattern. One-time items don't count here either.
  • Sales not shrinking more than 5%Keeps out value traps: stocks that look cheap because the business is dying.
  • Out of favorA flag, not a rule: the price is 25% or more below its 52-week high.
  • UndiscoveredA flag, not a rule: two or fewer Wall Street analysts publish estimates on it.

Figures come from each company's latest fiscal year and latest balance sheet filed with the SEC. Prices are from the last close.
