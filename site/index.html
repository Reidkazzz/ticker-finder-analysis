How to read the numbers

New to investing? These six ideas cover almost everything on this site.

Cheap is not the same as good. A stock can look cheap because the business is shrinking. That is why every screen also checks that the company is profitable, growing and not drowning in debt.
Price to sales
What the whole company costs divided by one year of sales. Under 2 means you pay less than $2 for every $1 it sells.
Net profit margin
The share of each sales dollar left as profit after every cost and tax. 8% means 8 cents of profit per dollar.
Debt to equity
What the company owes long term compared with what shareholders own. Lower is safer. Zero means no long-term debt.
Free cash flow
Cash left over after paying for operations and equipment. It is harder to fake than profit.
Form 4
A filing executives, directors and 10%+ owners must send the SEC within two business days of trading their company's stock.
Schedule 13D
Filed when an investor passes 5% ownership and may try to influence the company, for example by pushing for changes.
